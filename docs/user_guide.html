Chapter 1

What the app does

You type a company's name. The app gathers everything public about it and lays it out simply.

What you get

  • Today's price and a price chart (daily candles, or a simple line if you prefer)
  • A short AI summary and a one-line view: Looks good Mixed picture Be careful
  • Warning signs from the annual report — for example, if the auditor had concerns
  • New company announcements, explained in three simple points
  • What the company's bosses promised, and whether they kept it
  • Your own portfolio, with alerts when something changes

What it isn't

  • Not advice. It helps you understand a company; the decision is yours.
  • Not a trading app. It never connects to your broker or your money.
  • Not in the cloud. What you own stays on your computer.
Chapter 2

Set it up

About 15 minutes, once. The easiest way is to let an AI assistant do it for you.

The easy way: ask an AI assistant

Copy the message below into an AI assistant that can work on your computer — for example Claude Code, Claude Desktop, ChatGPT's desktop app, Codex or Cursor. It will install everything and tell you when it's ready. A chat-only assistant can't run anything itself, but it will walk you through each step.

Please set up the "Indian Stock Data Hub" app on my computer. I'm not
technical, so do every step yourself where you can, explain anything I
need to do in plain words, and check each step worked before moving on.

1. Check that Python 3.10 or newer is installed (python3 --version, or
   python --version on Windows). If not, install it from python.org. On
   Windows, tick "Add python.exe to PATH" in the installer.
2. Download the app into my Documents folder:
   git clone https://github.com/tapariapurv/indian-stock-data-hub.git
   (If git isn't available, download and unzip
   https://github.com/tapariapurv/indian-stock-data-hub/archive/refs/heads/main.zip)
3. In the app's folder, make a virtual environment and install the app:
   python3 -m venv .venv
   then  .venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
   (Windows: .venv\Scripts\python -m pip install -r requirements.txt)
4. Install Ollama, the free AI, from https://ollama.com/download and make
   sure it's running. Download a model that suits my computer's memory:
   "ollama pull gemma4:e2b" for 8 GB, "ollama pull gemma4:e4b" for 16 GB
   or more.
5. Start the app with  .venv/bin/python -m streamlit run app.py
   (Windows: .venv\Scripts\python -m streamlit run app.py)
   It opens at http://localhost:8501. In the app, go to Settings → AI,
   choose Ollama, press "Check it works", pick the model, and press
   "Save AI settings".
6. Ask me whether I'd like the app to start by itself when I log in. If
   yes, it's in Settings → Advanced → Speed & memory → Start automatically.
7. Finish by telling me, in two or three plain sentences, how to open the
   app next time.

Don't edit any of the app's files, and ask me before installing anything
that costs money (nothing here should).

Doing it yourself

1
Install Python from python.org (version 3.10 or newer). On Windows, tick "Add python.exe to PATH" on the first installer screen.
2
Download the app. On its GitHub page, click the green Code button → Download ZIP, and unzip it into your Documents folder.
3
Install it. Open Terminal (Mac) or PowerShell (Windows) in the app's folder and paste: 
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
On Windows, use python instead of python3, and .venv\Scripts\python instead of .venv/bin/python.
4
Start it — this is the only step you repeat next time: 
.venv/bin/python -m streamlit run app.py
Your browser opens the app. Keep the Terminal window open while you use it.

Turn on the free AI (recommended)

Everything works without AI except the summaries, Explain and Ask AI. To turn them on for free:

  1. Install Ollama from ollama.com/download and open it.
  2. In Terminal, download a model: ollama pull gemma4:e2b (or gemma4:e4b if your computer has 16 GB of memory or more).
  3. In the app, open Settings → AI, choose Ollama, press Check it works, pick the model, and press Save AI settings.
The AI then runs on your own computer: it's free and nothing is sent anywhere. Paid AI services (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) also work — pick one in Settings → AI and paste the key from its website. You can set a monthly spending limit in Settings → Spending.
Chapter 3

Home

Where the app opens. Everything you need at a glance.

  • Look up any company — click the search box and type part of a company's name or its symbol (small or capital letters, either works). Every company listed on the NSE is in the list; untick Include SMEs to leave out small and medium companies.
  • Worth today — what your stocks are worth, and how much that changed today.
  • Needs your attention — one line per stock when something happened: the AI's view changed, the company published something important, a promise wasn't kept, or there's a warning sign in its annual report. Hover over the ? for details, or press Open.
  • Your stocks — every stock you own. Tick the box beside one to open its page.

The first time, Home shows three steps to get started instead.

Chapter 4

A company's page

Everything about one company. Works for any listed company — you don't have to own it.

At the top: the price, what's coming up (results dates, dividends), and a price chart of daily candles (green up, red down) with the 50- and 200-day averages. Below the chart, the app tells you whether the share is cheaper or dearer than usual for this company.

Press Look into this company to have the app read its latest numbers, reports and news (about a minute) — do it again any time to refresh. To keep an eye on it, press Add to my portfolio. To be told when the price moves, press Tell me when the price moves.

SectionWhat it shows
SummaryThe AI's short summary, the key numbers, strengths, weaknesses and recent news.
ResultsSales and profit, quarter by quarter, with small trend charts.
Every numberEvery figure found in the company's reports, with the page it came from.
What changedWhat's different since the last time the app looked.
Promises kept?What the bosses promised investors, and whether later results show they kept it.
Reports & warningsWarning signs in the annual report, the latest announcements (press Explain for three simple points), and the reports the app read.
Past checksEvery earlier look at this company, so you can compare.

The warning signs it looks for

  • Auditor concern — the company's auditor wasn't fully happy with the accounts.
  • Accounting rules changed — the company changed how it counts things, which can flatter results.
  • Loans to related companies — money lent to companies linked to the owners jumped sharply.
  • Pending claim — tax demands or legal claims the company hasn't agreed to pay.
These are found by searching the report for tell-tale phrases — no AI involved — and each shows its page number so you can read it yourself.
Chapter 5

Portfolio

The stocks you own or want to follow.

  1. Press New account and give it any name, like My stocks. (Use more than one account to keep, say, each broker separate.)
  2. Press Add a stock, type its name, and optionally how many shares you own and what you paid. Have a list already? Use Add many at once from a spreadsheet.
  3. That's it. The app looks into each stock in the background and checks them again every few hours.
SectionWhat it shows
My stocksA card for each stock: price, trend, the AI's view, and your gain or loss.
How it's doingHow your stocks did over the past year, compared with the whole market.
Buys & sellsWrite down each buy and sell; the app works out your profit and yearly return.
SpreadHow your money is split across companies and industries, and a warning if too much sits in one.
AlertsEvery message the app has sent you.
Weekly summaryA one-page round-up of your week.
Chapter 6

Alerts

The app keeps watch so you don't have to.

In Settings → Alerts, choose how you'd like to hear (a pop-up on your Mac, email, or both) and what about:

  • The AI's view of a stock changes
  • A company publishes something important, or its new annual report has a warning sign
  • A promise wasn't kept
  • A price you're watching is reached

Press Send me a test to check it works. The app only sends alerts while it's running — to keep it running in the background, see Start automatically in chapter 8.

Setting up email (optional, 5 minutes)

Emails are sent from your own Google account, only ever to you.

  1. Go to script.google.com and press New project.
  2. Delete what's there and paste this, changing pick-a-password to any password you like: 
    const PASSWORD = 'pick-a-password';
function doPost(e) {
  const d = JSON.parse(e.postData.contents);
  if (d.token !== PASSWORD) return ContentService.createTextOutput('wrong password');
  MailApp.sendEmail({to: Session.getEffectiveUser().getEmail(),
                     subject: d.subject, body: d.text, htmlBody: d.html});
  return ContentService.createTextOutput('ok');
}
  3. Press Deploy → New deployment, choose Web app, set Execute as to Me and Who has access to Anyone, and press Deploy. Allow access when Google asks.
  4. Copy the Web app URL. In the app, open Settings → Alerts, tick Email, and paste the address and your password. Press Save alert settings, then Send me a test.
Chapter 7

More tools

Research and Ask AI are in the main menu; the rest are under More.

ToolWhat it's for
ResearchLook into several companies at once and download a spreadsheet or Word report.
Ask AIAsk anything in your own words — "Which of my stocks look weakest?" The answer shows where each fact came from.
ScreenerSearch every company listed on the NSE for ones that match what you want. Pick a ready-made search, like Cheap and profitable, or write one such as P/E < 15 AND Market Cap > 1000. Tick Include SMEs to add small and medium companies.
CompareTwo to four companies side by side; the best on each line is marked in green.
ArchiveSearch every report the app has downloaded, instantly and for free.
HistoryEvery earlier check of every company, and how its numbers moved over time.
In the Screener, price, company value, P/E, price-to-book, dividend yield, EPS and 1-year return are known for every listed company. ROE, ROCE, growth, the AI's view and promises are known only for companies the app has looked into — press Look into this company on a Stock page to add one. Compare works on companies the app has looked into.
Chapter 8

Make it yours

Everything is in Settings. Press the Save button in a section to keep a change.

SectionWhat you can change
Layout & lookMenu across the top or down the side · which page the app opens on · hide pages you don't use · which sections a company's page shows · simple or detailed price chart · colour, text size and spacing · a detailed (candles) or simple price chart · whether to track shares and prices · how often your stocks are re-checked.
AIWhich AI to use, or turn it off.
AlertsHow and when the app tells you things (chapter 6).
SpendingLimits for paid AI services. Free AI is never counted.

Want more detail? Turn on Show more details in Layout & look. It adds extra counts and figures, the original financial terms (like ROCE), AI costs and model names, file locations and full error messages.

Chapter 9

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
The app won't openCheck the Terminal window is still open. If you closed it, start the app again (chapter 2, step 4).
"Couldn't connect" in Settings → AIOpen the Ollama app, then press Check it works again.
No AI summaryTurn AI on in Settings → AI. If it's on, try again later or pick a different model.
"The search service is busy"Wait a few seconds and type again.
A company "couldn't load"Check the name, then try again in a minute.
The computer feels slowIn Settings → Advanced → Speed & memory, lower the limits. A smaller AI model also helps.
Anything elsePaste what you see into any AI assistant along with "I'm using the Indian Stock Data Hub app" — it can usually tell you what to do.
Chapter 10

Words you might see

Symbol
A company's short name on the stock exchange, like TCS or INFY.
Price / earnings (P/E)
How many years of today's profit the share price equals. Lower is cheaper.
Return on equity (ROE)
Profit made for every ₹100 the owners have put in. Higher is better.
Return on capital (ROCE)
Profit made for every ₹100 the business uses, including borrowed money.
Dividend
Cash the company pays its shareholders.
Company value (market cap)
The price of all its shares added together.
Annual report
The company's yearly report, including its audited accounts.
Investor call
Where the bosses talk to investors after results. The app reads the written record.
Announcement
A notice every listed company must send the stock exchange when something happens.
Auditor
An outside firm that checks the company's accounts each year.
Nifty 50
The 50 biggest companies in India — a way to see how "the market" did.
Tokens
How AI services measure text, roughly one word each. Only matters for paid AI.
